What we do

We help deep tech ideas become companies. Engineers build the technology, and we test it with the people who would pay for it.

  1. Step 01

    Test the idea

    We find out who has the problem, how often it happens and what it costs them. If there's no company worth building, we find that out early, while changing direction is still cheap.

    • Customer interviews
    • Market research
    • Business model
    • Technical feasibility
    • Team formation

  2. Step 02

    Build the prototype

    Our engineers find the riskiest technical question and build to answer it first. Then the prototype leaves the workbench and goes into a real site, like a mine, a farm or a factory.

    • Robotics and autonomy
    • Electronics and embedded systems
    • Mechanical design and CAD
    • Software and AI
    • Pilot testing

  3. Step 03

    Find the first customers

    We work out who buys, who signs off, and what it takes to turn a conversation into a paid pilot. Then we get the team ready to talk to investors.

    • Pilot design
    • Founder-led sales
    • Pricing
    • Partnerships
    • Investor readiness

Going further

For some ventures we join the founding team itself, and fill whichever gap is biggest: engineering, sales, hiring or operations.

Everything we help with

Seven ways in.

  1. We look for evidence that a real problem exists and that someone cares enough to solve it. We talk to users, buyers and decision makers about how they deal with the problem today and what it costs them. Then we push past “that’s interesting” towards stronger signs, like a trial, or someone committing money or time.

  2. Our engineers turn ideas into prototypes and working systems. We find the technical question with the most uncertainty and build to answer it first. The first prototype doesn’t need to look good. It needs to answer something important.

  3. A system that works on the workbench can behave very differently in a mine, a warehouse or a factory. We set up pilots around measurable outcomes, so the startup and the customer both know what success looks like.

  4. Deep tech ventures often fail in the gap between a promising technology and a viable company. We help engineers turn what they can build into commercial value, and help customers and investors see what the technology can realistically deliver.

  5. We find a repeatable path from the product to the customer. That means working out who the first customers should be, who makes the buying decision, and how a first conversation becomes a pilot, a contract or a long-term customer.

  6. We break big ideas into specific assumptions and test them before serious time or money goes in. The aim isn’t to make an idea look investable. It’s to find out whether there’s a company worth building, and if there is, help build it.

  7. For selected ventures we work alongside the founding team instead of advising from outside. That can cover engineering, customers, partnerships, hiring and operations, wherever the company’s biggest gap is.
